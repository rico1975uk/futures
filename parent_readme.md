# Futures: a guide for parents

Futures is a free website that helps you choose a secondary school for your child in the Bristol area.

**Website:** https://rico1975uk.github.io/futures/

It takes about 10 minutes. You do not need to sign up or give your email address.

---

## What Futures does

- You tell it about your child, and what is important to your family.
- You choose the schools you are thinking about.
- It gives you a **report** about how well each school might suit your child.
- It gives you **questions to ask** when you visit schools.

Futures helps you think. It does **not** choose a school for you. You know your child best.

---

## How to use it

There are 8 steps. **Every question is optional.** If you are not sure, you can skip it.

**1. Choose schools**
- Type the first part of your postcode (for example **BS7**). Do not type your full postcode or address.
- Look at the list of schools. Click **Add to shortlist** for the schools you are interested in. You can choose up to 8.

**2. Describe your child**
- Read each sentence. Choose how much it is like your child, from "Not like them" to "Very like them".
- Think about how your child is **most of the time**, not on a very good or very bad day.
- There are no right or wrong answers.

**3. Wellbeing and the move to Year 7**
- A few questions about how your child feels about starting secondary school, and about friendships.

**4. Learning**
- How your child is doing in reading and writing, maths and science, and what they enjoy. "Not sure" is fine.

**5. What matters to you**
- Move the sliders to show what is important to your family, for example good exam results, or a short journey to school.

**6. Your visits**
- Fill this in after you visit a school. You can come back to it later.
- **Ask your child what they think first**, before you tell them what you think.
- Then answer some simple questions about what you saw, for example: "Were the lessons calm?"
- Then give your own overall opinion.

**7. Your report**
- Click **Generate my report**.
- You can print it, or save it as a PDF, with **Print / save as PDF**.

**8. Dig deeper with AI (optional)**
- See "Using AI to find out more" below.

**Your answers are saved on your device.** If you close the page, you can come back later and continue on the same device and web browser.

## What the report tells you

- **About your child:** what your answers suggest your child needs from a school, for example strong support when starting Year 7, or more challenging work.
- **The move to Year 7:** things that may make the move harder or easier for your child.
- **Each school:** a rating, with reasons:
  - **Strong match**
  - **Good match**
  - **Mixed match**
  - **Weaker match**
- **What drove this rating:** a table that shows exactly why a school got its rating.
- **Where your impressions and the evidence disagree:** if you liked a school but the information about it is not so good, or the other way round, the report tells you, and suggests what to ask.
- **Questions to ask** on your school visits.
- **Other schools** near you that you might want to look at.

If you do not answer the questions about your child, the report only compares the schools using public information. It will tell you this.

## How it works

Futures uses two kinds of information:

1. **What you tell it** about your child and your family.
2. **Public information about schools**, from the Department for Education and Ofsted (the school inspection service). For example:
   - GCSE exam results
   - Ofsted inspection results
   - how many pupils often miss school
   - how many pupils are in each year
   - whether the school has a sixth form or is a faith school.

Futures looks at what your child needs, and checks which schools are strong in those areas. For example, if your child worries a lot, it gives more importance to how well a school looks after pupils.

**Some important points:**
- **Exam results mostly show which children go to a school**, not only how good the teaching is. Futures compares each school with schools that have similar pupils, to make this fairer.
- **The questions about your child are not a psychological test.** They are a simple way to think about what your child needs. Children also change a lot between age 10 and 14.
- **Your visit opinions do not change the rating.** This is on purpose. The rating shows what the public information says. Your opinions are shown next to it, so you can compare the two. If they are different, it is worth finding out why.
- **The ratings are a guide, not a fact.** The way Futures adds things up is a careful judgement. It has not been tested against how children actually got on at each school.

## Your data and privacy

- **Your answers stay on your device.** Futures runs only in your web browser. Your answers are **not** sent to us or to anyone else.
- **There is no account and no login.** We do not ask for your name, email or address.
- **We only ask for the first part of your postcode** (for example BS7). This is used only to work out distances to schools.
- **Your child's name is optional.** It is only used to make the report more personal on your screen.
- **Your answers are saved in your web browser** so you can come back later. If you use a shared or public computer, click **Clear all answers** at the end.
- **If you print or download your report,** you decide who sees it.

## Using AI to find out more (optional)

After your report, Futures gives you some ready-made questions (called "prompts") for an AI assistant such as **Claude**, **Gemini** or **ChatGPT**.

An AI assistant can search the internet for things Futures does not know, for example:
- how far away from the school children lived when they were offered a place last year
- what happens when children start in Year 7
- the school's rules about behaviour and mobile phones
- clubs and activities
- open evening dates.

**How to use it:**
1. Click **Copy prompt**.
2. Open your AI assistant.
3. Paste the prompt in, and send it.

**Please remember:**
- **Anything you paste into an AI assistant is sent to that company.** Their privacy rules apply, not ours.
- You can choose what goes into the prompts. By default, your postcode area and visit notes **are** included, and your child's name and wellbeing details are **not**.
- **AI can make mistakes.** Always check important facts with the school's own website or the council.

## What Futures does not do

- **It cannot tell you if you will get a place.** Places depend on each school's admission rules, for example where you live and whether a brother or sister goes there.
- **It does not include private (independent) schools.**
- **It does not give advice about special educational needs (SEND).** If your child has an EHCP or special educational needs, please talk to your child's current school and your local council.
- **It is not an application.** You still need to apply through your council.

---

## Applying for a school place

- **Apply through your home council**, even if the school is in a different council area. For most of Bristol this is Bristol City Council.
- In England, the deadline to apply for a secondary school place is usually **31 October**, in the year before your child starts.
- Offers are usually sent on **1 March**.
- You can list several schools in order of preference. Check your council's website for the exact dates and rules.
- Official information: https://www.gov.uk/apply-for-secondary-school-place

## Tips for choosing a school

- **Visit on a normal school day if you can,** not only at an open evening. Open evenings are planned to show the school at its best.
- **Listen to your child.** Children who feel they had a say often settle in better.
- **Try this question with your child:** "Imagine it is Year 8 and things are not going well. What went wrong?" It can help you both think about worries.
- **Most children settle in well.** Even children who are worried in the summer are usually settled by the end of October.
- **There is no perfect school,** and no choice is final. Children can change schools if they need to.

## Is the information up to date?

The school information was collected in **October 2026**, using the **2025–26 GCSE results**. Schools change, so always check the school's own website, the latest Ofsted report (https://reports.ofsted.gov.uk/) and the Department for Education website (https://www.compare-school-performance.service.gov.uk/).
